# Print labels

![The print screen](/screens/print-light.png)

The **Print** page has your labels on the left, the selected label in the middle
and the print queue on the right.

## Choose the size and stock

The bar at the top sets the printer, label size, stock (paper colour and finish)
and ribbon colour. The preview shows the exact dots the printer gets, in those
colours. The dashed line marks what the printer can reach.

**All sizes** shows the same label on other sizes the design suits. Click one to
switch.

## Before you print

Under the preview, labebal lists anything that would print badly: text that is
too small or cut off, barcodes too fine to scan, or something outside the
printable area. Each warning says what to change.

## The queue

Press **+** on a label to queue it, set how many copies you want, then **Print**.
You can pause, resume or cancel a run, and continue from a given label after a
paper jam. **Save as run** keeps the queue to print again later under **Stock
runs**.

Labels are numbered one by one when they use a counter or a running number.

## Several printers

Connect more than one printer and each queued label goes to the printer whose
roll fits. Printer settings, print position and darkness are kept per printer in
**Settings > Printers**.

| Printer | Connects by |
| --- | --- |
| Niimbot N1 / B18, M2 | Bluetooth or USB |
| Brother PT-D610BT | Its Bluetooth serial port |
| Zebra and other ZPL printers | The network, through the labebal server |

If the print sits too high or to one side, print the test pattern in
**Settings > Printers** and adjust the offset.

## On a phone

![Quick print on a phone](/screens/quick-light.png){width=320}

**Quick print** is a touch screen for printing on the go. On Android, install
labebal as an app from the browser menu: it keeps printing labels it has seen
even when offline.

**Boards** go one step further: a saved set of labels, each at its own size. Tap
one and it prints.

## History

Every print is recorded with a picture of what came out, who printed it and on
which printer. Reprint from there, either as it was printed or with the current
design. Mark misprints as void rather than deleting them.
